Executive summary

What was built, what the verified data shows, what it cannot show, and how a trader uses it. Snapshot 2026-11-21 00:55:22 UTC. Every claim below links to its official source.

Open the live dashboard

1 · What was built

A single, static, dependency-free dashboard for Kalshi's official demo exchange, published on GitHub Pages. It tracks the highest-dollar live events in exactly the five requested topics — gas prices, elections, culture, crypto and golf — follows each market until it resolves yes or no, and turns price action into a live alerting system.

  • Two scrolling chats. The left pane posts every tracked contract that moves at least $0.10 in either direction. The right pane posts only the moves the published ROI model likes. Both carry the official settlement source that decides the outcome, so a swing can be investigated in one click.
  • Resolution tracking. Every market is followed from active through determined to finalized; the result is shown in the tables and announced in the chat.
  • Price and liquidity. A time-series chart with selectable 30-second to 24-hour windows, a price axis, and an order-book ladder that answers "how many contracts are available at this price".
  • Simulated trading. Five published strategies trade automatically against live prices, settle on resolution, and are analysed by strategy and by topic so a new strategy can be judged on results.
  • Your demo account, read-only. Paste your own demo API key and the site reads your balance, positions, fills and settlements. The key is held in memory only, and the site has no order-placement button.

3 · Key verified facts

  • Public market data needs no authentication; only balances, positions and orders need signing. Market data
  • The demo endpoints in the brief match Kalshi's documentation exactly, and demo credentials are separate from production credentials. Demo environment
  • Markets resolve with yes, no or scalar; the winning side is paid $1.00 per contract. Settlement · Lifecycle
  • The WebSocket feed requires API-key authentication during the handshake, so a keyless static site must poll REST — and REST quotes can lag the exchange by a few seconds. WebSocket
  • Demo prices and behaviour may not reflect real markets, and demo funds are mock funds. Demo environment

4 · What it cannot show

  • Why a contract moved. The Kalshi API publishes no news feed. The dashboard links the event's official settlement source as a candidate explanation, and says so rather than implying a proven cause.
  • Sub-minute history from the API. Candlestick periods are 1, 60 or 1440 minutes; seconds resolution exists only while a page is open.
  • A real portfolio without a key. No credential is committed, so the tracked portfolio is simulated; the demo-account page can read your own account if you paste your own key.
  • Anything outside the five topics. The demo exchange lists many more categories; they are out of scope by the brief, and the full taxonomy is linked from API reference.

Every limitation is listed with its source on Flagged for Review.

5 · What a trader should do with it

  1. Open the dashboard and leave the left chat running. It is the cheapest way to notice a $0.10 move in a contract you care about.
  2. Use the right-hand pane to cut the noise: it only shows the contracts with liquidity, a tight spread and real volume.
  3. Open the contract, check the chart, the ladder and the settlement rules, and confirm the official source that decides it.
  4. Record the trade on the portfolio page; let the auto-trader run the published strategies and read the outcome on analysis.
  5. Check every number against the source link before acting. That is the point of the source column.
