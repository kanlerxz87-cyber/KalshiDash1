What this session changed, and what it recommends next

Five rows used to top the high-ROI chat that could not have been traded at the price shown — a “$0.01 → $0.97” print on a market whose published YES ask was $0.01, and a “$0.803 → $0.003” print with no YES bid at all. A trader reading that pane would have been looking at noise. Two changes fix the class of problem, not just those rows: a follow-through gate (the quote has to sit where the swing ended, or the row is scored 0 and capped at 0.30) and contract counts beside every quote (a $0.97 ask × 0 contracts is not an offer). Both are checked by tests that were verified to fail on the old code. Details and every source link are in F-71.

Recommended next, in the order that would most change what a trader can decide:

  1. Show the executable size on the chart. A price line says nothing about whether $0.10 of movement can be bought. Plotting the touch size (already published as yes_bid_size_fp / yes_ask_size_fp) as a second, low-contrast band would make “real move or empty book” a glance instead of a click.
  2. Cap alerts per ticker per day, not just per 15 minutes. One contract produced most of the recorded feed (24 of 38 rows). The chat is readable today only because of the collapse rules; a daily cap would keep one flapping ticker from owning the pane.
  3. Time-to-resolution on every row. close_time is already captured; sorting the panes by “how long does this have left” answers the question a trader actually asking about a stale-looking market.
  4. Persist the alert history outside the repo. data/live/feed.json is capped at 200 rows and is rewritten on every job run, so today’s alerts quietly push out yesterday’s. An append-only CSV artifact would make the “analyse the results by trade category” part of the brief answerable over weeks rather than hours.

What would make the charts simpler, not busier

  • One number per chart — the current price and the change — with the axis doing the rest. No dual axes, no gauges.
  • Gold markers only on observed ≥ $0.10 changes, with an adjacent sourced row — no absolute 10-cent/90-cent threshold lines.
  • Sparklines in the tables instead of full charts: the full chart belongs on the market page only.
  • Colour used only for direction and for status, so a green bar always means the same thing.
  • Every chart captioned with the endpoint and the window it came from, so a reader can reproduce it.
